Wenxin Chen陈文新

I am an undergraduate at the SJTU–UM Joint Institute studying Electrical and Computer Engineering (GPA 3.78/4.0, Top 10%), transferring to the University of Michigan, Ann Arbor for Computer Science in Fall 2026. I am broadly interested in how learned representations can support grounded, adaptive behavior — connecting perception to action through structured internal models of the world.

My recent work probes the limits of what targeted supervision can do for small models: in CerebraGloss (ICLR 2026), instruction-tuning on domain-specific neural-signal data let a 3B model outperform GPT-4o by 10 points on clinical EEG reasoning, showing that task-specific supervision can substitute for scale. This raises a question I find central to embodied systems: what internal representations does a model need to act reliably under partial observability — and how efficiently can they be acquired?

I am particularly drawn to world models and continual learning — how an agent builds, updates, and leverages a predictive model of its environment to plan over long horizons and generalize across distribution shift. I am open to internship and research assistant opportunities.
