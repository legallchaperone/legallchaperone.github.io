Wenxin Chen

I am an undergraduate at the SJTU–UM Joint Institute studying Electrical and Computer Engineering (GPA 3.78/4.0, Top 10%), transferring to the University of Michigan, Ann Arbor for Computer Science in Fall 2026. My research interests lie in the foundations of language models — particularly understanding when and why targeted training strategies (such as instruction tuning, reinforcement learning, and distillation) enable small models to close the gap with much larger ones.

This interest grew from my work on CerebraGloss (ICLR 2026), where we showed a 3B instruction-tuned model outperforming GPT-4o by 10 points on domain-specific reasoning, and continues through my work on meta-learning under distribution shift (BrainTune, under review). I serve as a Teaching Assistant for Honors Mathematics and am building deeper foundations in optimization and statistical learning theory.

I am also broadly interested in agent reasoning, agent memory, and continual learning — how autonomous systems can accumulate, organize, and selectively retain knowledge over extended interactions. I am open to internship and research assistant opportunities.

Projects

NanoBrain ↗ GitHub

A minimal CLI agent that combines OpenCode (running in Docker) with a credit-assignment memory system. The agent learns which memories were useful by tracking their contribution to successful outcomes, so its context self-optimizes over time.

Claude Docker Node.js MCP Memory Systems
  • Implements credit-assignment scoring (exponential moving average) to rank memories by their contribution to task success; low-performers are pruned during consolidation cycles.
  • Two operational modes: prompt-level strategy adjustments via STRATEGY.md, and direct code self-modification of memory lifecycle logic.
  • /good and /bad commands let users directly reinforce or penalize memory contributions; full history is git-tracked for rollback.
