1. Stable matching #

  • nnn women, nnn men, each ranks the other side.
  • Blocking pair: (i,j)(i,j)(i,j), (i′,j′)(i',j')(i′,j′) matched, but iii and j′j'j′ prefer each other.
  • Stable: no blocking pair.

2. Gale–Shapley (deferred acceptance) #

  1. Each free woman proposes to her best man who hasn't rejected her.
  2. Each man tentatively keeps his best proposal, rejects the rest.
  3. Repeat until all matched.
  • Terminates: ≤n2\le n^2≤n2 proposals.
  • Stable, by contradiction: suppose (w,m)(w,m)(w,m) blocks.
    • www proposed to mmm earlier.
    • mmm rejected her for someone better.
    • His partners only improve ⇒ mmm doesn't prefer www.
  • Proposer-optimal, receiver-pessimal among all stable matchings.

3. Costs and linear assignment #

  • Optimality ⇒ no profitable swap: c(i,j)+c(i′,j′)≤c(i,j′)+c(i′,j)c(i,j) + c(i',j') \le c(i,j') + c(i',j)c(i,j)+c(i′,j′)≤c(i,j′)+c(i′,j).
    • Necessary only; full condition = cyclic monotonicity.
  • Linear assignment: min⁡T permutation∑ic(i,T(i))\min_{T \text{ permutation}} \sum_i c(i, T(i))minT permutation​∑i​c(i,T(i)).
  • n!n!n! options.

5. Monge and Kantorovich #

Monge:

min⁡T#p=qE[c(X,T(X))],X∼p \min_{\mathbf{T}_\# p = q} \mathbb{E}\big[c(\mathsf{X}, \mathbf{T}(\mathsf{X}))\big], \quad \mathsf{X}\sim p T#​p=qmin​E[c(X,T(X))],X∼p
  • Soil ppp, holes qqq.
  • T\mathbf{T}T moves each pile to one hole.
  • A feasible T\mathbf{T}T may not exist (cannot split mass).

Kantorovich:

min⁡T#p=qE[c(X,T(X))]  ≥  min⁡X∼p, Y∼qE[c(X,Y)] \min_{\mathbf{T}_\# p = q} \mathbb{E}\big[c(\mathsf{X}, \mathbf{T}(\mathsf{X}))\big] \;\ge\; \min_{\mathsf{X}\sim p,\ \mathsf{Y}\sim q} \mathbb{E}\big[c(\mathsf{X}, \mathsf{Y})\big] T#​p=qmin​E[c(X,T(X))]≥X∼p, Y∼qmin​E[c(X,Y)]

Coupling: (X,Y)∼π(\mathsf{X}, \mathsf{Y})\sim\pi(X,Y)∼π, where the joint π\piπ has marginals ppp and qqq.

  • Deterministic pairing: y=Tx\mathbf{y} = \mathbf{T}\mathbf{x}y=Tx.
  • Stochastic pairing: π(y∣x)\pi(\mathbf{y}|\mathbf{x})π(y∣x).
  • ≥\ge≥: every T\mathbf{T}T gives a coupling (X,T(X))(\mathsf{X}, \mathbf{T}(\mathsf{X}))(X,T(X)) ⇒ min over a bigger set.
  • At optimality π(y∣x)\pi(\mathbf{y}|\mathbf{x})π(y∣x) can still be deterministic.

7. Vertices = pure plans (Birkhoff–von Neumann) #

Vertex of a convex set: not a weighted average of two other distinct points.

Pure ⇒ vertex:

  • Suppose P=12(A+B)P = \frac12(A+B)P=21​(A+B).
  • Zeros of PPP force zeros in A,BA, BA,B.
  • Row sums force the remaining entries ⇒ A=B=PA = B = PA=B=P.

Non-pure ⇒ not vertex:

  • Fractional entry ⇒ another in its row ⇒ another in that column ⇒ … ⇒ a cycle.
  • Alternate ±1\pm1±1 on the cycle ⇒ DDD with zero row/column sums.
  • π=12[(π+εD)+(π−εD)]\pi = \frac12[(\pi+\varepsilon D) + (\pi-\varepsilon D)]π=21​[(π+εD)+(π−εD)].

Conclusion: LP optimum is at a vertex ⇒ relaxed optimum is a permutation.

Hungarian algorithm (exact linear assignment):

  • Subtracting a constant from a row/column doesn't change the optimal permutation.
  • Steps:
    1. Row-reduce, then column-reduce.
    2. Cover all zeros with the fewest lines.
    3. nnn lines ⇒ done.
    4. Else: subtract the smallest uncovered kkk from uncovered entries, add kkk at intersections, go to 2.
  • Reductions ui,vju_i, v_jui​,vj​ are dual variables: cij−ui−vj≥0c_{ij} - u_i - v_j \ge 0cij​−ui​−vj​≥0.

18. K-means as Wasserstein projection #

Lloyd's algorithm:

  1. Assign each point to the nearest center.
  2. Move each center to the mean of its points.
  3. Repeat (monotone, local optimum).
